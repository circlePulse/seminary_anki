## The section in brief

- **Every muʿrab ism is munṣarif or ghayr munṣarif.** Munṣarif is the default: the word takes tanwīn and all three
  endings.
- **A ghayr munṣarif ism never takes tanwīn or a kasrah.** In jarr it takes a fatḥah instead: marartu bi-Aḥmada.
  The kasrah comes back when the word has al- or is muḍāf.
- **A word becomes ghayr munṣarif through one strong cause or two weak ones.**
  - **Strong causes:** the plural shaped like masājid or mafātīḥ, and the alif of taʾnīth, as in ṣaḥrāʾ and dhikrā.
  - **Weak causes pair up:** one of meaning (a name or a descriptor) with one of form. The six of form are a tāʾ
    marbūṭah or feminine meaning, being non-Arabic, being a compound, an added alif and nūn, a verb's pattern, and
    ʿadl.
- **Examples:** Aḥmad is a name on a verb's pattern. Fāṭimah is a name with a tāʾ marbūṭah. Ibrāhīm is a
  non-Arabic name. ʿUthmān is a name with an added alif and nūn.

## Check this first

1. **A tāʾ marbūṭah is not a strong cause.** Your note lists "a round ta" beside the two alifs under the strong
   cause. A word like madrasatun takes tanwīn, so the tāʾ only works with being a name, as your own weak-cause list
   says. Only the alif of taʾnīth is strong alone. The cards and your notes now say so.
2. **"Words that were originally made up of one word"** should be two words fused into one name, like Ḥaḍramawt.
3. **Added because the notes don't say it:** what ghayr munṣarif does to a word (no tanwīn, a fatḥah in jarr), and
   that the kasrah returns with al- or as a muḍāf. Without these the causes have nothing to explain.
4. **Also added from what I know:**
   - An example for every cause, the ten "why is this word ghayr munṣarif" cards, and the Qurʾān examples.
   - Why Nūḥ and Lūṭ take tanwīn though they're non-Arabic names.
   - That "ṣarf" in mamnūʿ min al-ṣarf means tanwīn.
   - That kam can exclaim as well as ask, and that kayta and dhayta stand for words or events where kam and kadhā
     stand for amounts.

## Decisions you should know about

- **Your "'al'alaamah"** is al-ʿalamiyyah, being a proper name.
- **The teacher's framework is kept:** strong and weak causes, with the weak ones split into meaning and form. It
  covers the same nine causes the books list.
- **All Tier 1.**
